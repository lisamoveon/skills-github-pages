used to think building good habits meant waking up at 5 a.m., sticking to strict schedules, and never slipping up. Every time I missed a day, I’d give up entirely and tell myself I’d start again next week. Sound familiar?

Over the past few months, I’ve changed my approach. Instead of big, scary goals, I’ve been testing tiny, low-effort habits. I don’t aim to read 50 pages a day — I just open my book and read one paragraph. I don’t force a 40-minute workout; I put on my shoes and walk around the block once.

The magic is removing the pressure. These actions are so easy I can’t make an excuse to skip them. Most days, once I start, I naturally keep going. On tough days, that one small step is still enough to keep the habit alive.

Progress doesn’t have to be fast or impressive. Showing up, even in the tiniest way, beats perfect plans that never get started.
